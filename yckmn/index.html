About the song

You can keep my name.

Sometimes walking away doesn't require hatred. Sometimes, you simply stop showing up.

“you can keep my name” is a sarcastic take on outgrowing a relationship with someone who constantly creates problems, demands emotional attention, and refuses to take responsibility for their own behavior.

The song isn't necessarily about a romantic relationship. The “you” can be a former lover, a close friend, or simply someone who once had a meaningful place in your life but gradually became more exhausting than meaningful.

The verses are built around deliberately insincere apologies. “I'm sorry” becomes less an expression of regret and more a sarcastic way of pointing out the absurdity of being expected to care about problems that are no longer the narrator's responsibility. The repeated “Get over yourself” chorus turns that frustration into a blunt, almost playful boundary.

“You can keep my name. You can remember me, talk about me, whatever. But you don't get to keep having access to me just because we used to be close.”HAFYZ · Artist statement

The bridge, “And I could be your someone / But you'd hate him too,” is where resentment becomes clarity. The narrator no longer feels responsible for fixing the relationship or becoming the person this “you” needs.

At its core, the song is about boundaries, detachment, and knowing when to stop being someone's emotional safety net. Walking away doesn't always require hatred.

The artwork

Still there. Not participating.

Aware, not isolated.

Surrounded by a group of people caught up in their own conversations, HAFYZ is the only person looking directly into the camera. He isn't angry or isolated. He is simply aware.

“Everyone is looking at someone else, talking and laughing, and I'm looking straight at the camera. I'm still there, but I'm not participating in the drama anymore.”HAFYZ · On the artwork
